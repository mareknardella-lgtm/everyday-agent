Direct answers, no marketing language. If your question isn't here, the agent itself can explain any decision.

The home domain organizes maintenance, utilities, supplies and warranty reminders. In this prototype it only stores local reminders; it does not contact contractors, place orders or control devices.
The prototype does not connect to banks or execute payments. A future money connector would require explicit confirmation, least-privilege access, transaction and monthly caps, audit records and a jurisdiction-specific review.
It can organize operational reminders such as appointments or prescriptions in the local prototype. It does not diagnose, recommend treatment or replace a healthcare professional.
It organizes shopping lists, bookings and deliveries. In this prototype it stores local items only; real bookings, cancellations and external messages are not performed.
The Dynamic Trust Engine assigns a continuous score from 0 to 100 for every combination of action, counterparty and context. New combinations start at 20/100 (cautious). Approvals increase the score, rejections decrease it, and hesitation keeps it low. The score decays over time if there are no interactions. Money, health and legal documents always have absolute safety caps.
Yes. Every automatic action (Level 1) can be explained on demand. Ask "why did you pay this bill without asking me?" and you get the exact rule, trust score, and context that was applied. Transparency is a core design principle.
During the first 14 days, the agent notifies more than necessary to learn your real preferences. It tells you explicitly: "I'm still calibrating, expect a few more notifications this week." After this period, it reduces notifications as trust builds.

The prototype does not provide a legal guarantee or insurance promise. It records local actions and offers local correction controls, but responsibility, remediation, refunds, limitations and escalation must be defined in final Terms and applicable contracts. Do not rely on the prototype for consequential decisions.
