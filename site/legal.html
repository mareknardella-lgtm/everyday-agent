Pre-launch disclosure

Legal and privacy information.

This page describes the development prototype. It is not final Terms of Service, a Privacy Notice, legal advice, a warranty or a certification of compliance.

1. What this demo is

Everyday Agent currently runs as a local development demo. It does not connect to banks, email, calendars, healthcare systems or vendors. It does not perform payments, send messages, sign contracts, place bookings, control devices or provide medical, legal, financial or tax advice.

2. Data in the prototype

Dashboard state is stored in the browser’s local storage on the device where the demo is used. Do not enter real financial, health, legal, authentication, identity or family-sensitive information. The public site may request hosted fonts; this demo does not send dashboard state to an external service.

Local controls include export, pause, manual confirmation and deletion of browser state. They are product controls, not a substitute for the identity verification, response tracking, legal holds, retention jobs or data-subject procedures required by a production service.

3. User rights and consent

A production operator must identify the controller or processor for each processing flow, state the purpose and lawful basis, minimize data, define retention, list recipients and provide the applicable access, rectification, erasure, restriction, portability and objection workflows. Consent must be specific, informed, recorded, revocable and separate from acceptance of general terms where the law requires it.

4. EU and Italy review

Before offering the service in the European Union or Italy, qualified privacy and legal professionals must review GDPR Articles 6, 9, 13 and 14 as applicable, profiling and automated-decision disclosures, DPIA requirements, processor contracts, international transfers, Italian consumer and subscription rules, accessibility obligations and any financial or health-sector regulation. The current prototype makes no claim that this review is complete.

5. United States review

US requirements vary by state, sector and user type. Before launch, counsel must assess consumer-protection and advertising claims, California and other state privacy rights, health-data rules, minors, biometric data, communications, payments, financial regulation, electronic signatures, breach notices, contract terms, dispute resolution and insurance. The prototype makes no claim of HIPAA, CCPA/CPRA or other US compliance.

6. Safety and responsibility

Trust scores are not legal authorization. Sensitive, irreversible, fraudulent or high-impact actions must override convenience and trust. Future integrations require least-privilege credentials, explicit confirmation, server-side authorization, caps, idempotency, auditability, monitoring and a kill switch. The prototype does not promise error-free operation, availability, refunds, insurance coverage, a particular outcome or absence of damage.

7. Before production

The operator must publish versioned Terms, Privacy Notice, cookie notice where applicable, pricing and refund terms, support and incident contacts, accessibility statement, subprocessors, retention schedule, security controls, deletion/export process, family and minor policy, AI limitations and a jurisdiction-specific complaint route. A real legal entity, address, contact channel, effective date and governing law must replace this disclosure.

Important: “privacy-first”, “trust-first” and “local” describe design goals or the current demo scope. They are not promises of legal compliance, security, confidentiality, availability or immunity from claims. Do not rely on this prototype for consequential decisions.
